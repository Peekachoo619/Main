Strategy

Sell the number, not the dryer

Every premium dryer claims to protect your hair. Almost none can show it. Iris can: the temperature floats in front of you while you dry. That readout is the most ownable asset you have, so the ad treats it as proof rather than a feature. When the viewer sees the number, the claim becomes believable.

“Most dryers never tell you how hot they are. Iris shows you.”

There is a second layer worth using. In Greek myth Iris is the messenger goddess of the rainbow, the one who carries light between worlds. We borrow that idea visually with a restrained spectrum of light passing through the transparent display. It gives the brand a signature look that stays consistent across every future ad.

Hook inside 1.5 seconds

Feeds are scrolled at thumb speed. Each hook opens on motion, a question or a pattern break before the viewer can decide to leave.

Product on screen by second 3

Ads that show the product early build recall even for people who never finish watching. Iris appears in every hook within the first three seconds.

Built for sound off

Most people watch Reels and Stories muted, so captions and on-screen type carry the whole story. Sound design adds to it for TikTok, where audio is on by default.

Native first, premium second

Open with something that feels like it belongs in the feed, then move into a polished product world. The contrast makes the premium section feel earned.

One message

Visible heat means control, and control means healthier-looking hair. Lightweight feel and design are supporting proof, not separate stories.

Modular by design

Hooks, body and end card are separate modules, so new hooks and offers can be swapped in later without rebuilding the ad.

The visual recipe

Four styles in one ad, each with its own job

Real footage

Your product, filmed or photographed. Every close-up of Iris and every macro of the display comes from real plates. This is where accuracy is carried.

AI scenes

The world around the product. Morning bathrooms full of Perth light, hair movement, coastal exteriors and model shots, generated at a scale a physical shoot couldn't match on this budget.

Compositing

The bridge between the two. Real Iris plates are tracked, lit and colour-matched into AI scenes, so when someone holds or uses the dryer, it is your dryer.

Motion graphics

The readout and the message. The display's live temperature, kinetic captions, spectrum light sweeps and the end card. The numbers are designed, not generated, so they are always legible and correct.

Editing

Pace, rhythm and sound. Beat-matched cuts, match cuts between scenes, a unified colour grade, and sound design (airflow, clicks, a warm tonal rise as temperature climbs).

Product accuracy protocol

Iris is never left to AI’s imagination

You asked whether I'm comfortable keeping the dryer accurate, including with real footage and compositing. Yes. It's the core of how I work on physical products. AI video models tend to redraw products, soften logos and invent buttons, so the plan is designed around never trusting them with the product itself.

1

Real plates for close-ups

All hero, macro and display shots use your real footage or photography, cleaned up, relit and graded. No AI generation in these frames.

2

Composite, don't generate

In hand-held and in-use scenes, the AI scene is generated around a stand-in, then the real Iris is tracked, masked and composited over it frame by frame.

3

Readouts are graphics

The Floating Display's numbers are rebuilt as motion graphics in your display's real font and colour, and placed inside the transparent panel with glass reflections.

Frame-by-frame QC before anything reaches you

  • Silhouette and proportions
  • Logo position and legibility
  • Colourway matched to your hex values
  • Display transparency and digit accuracy
  • Nozzle, buttons and cable details
  • Hand grip and scale
  • No morphing between frames

How we test them

Launch

All three hooks in one ad set with equal budget, running 3–5 days.

Read

Hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions), hold rate to 15 seconds, CTR and cost per purchase.

Keep

Scale the winning hook and pause the others once there's enough data.

Iterate

Build 2 new variations of the winner. This is where a regular working relationship pays off.

Workflow

From brief to test-ready files

  1. Discovery and asset intake

    I review your scripts, product files, brand guide and approved claims, and audit the footage for what can be used as hero plates.

  2. Creative lock

    Final script, storyboard and three hooks, signed off by you before any generation starts. This step saves the most time later.

  3. Product plate preparation

    Cut-outs, clean-up, relighting and colour-matching of your product images and footage, plus rebuilding the display readout as graphics.

  4. AI scene generation

    Lifestyle scenes generated with reference frames for consistent lighting, talent and set. Many takes per shot, curated down to the best.

  5. Compositing and product lock

    The real Iris is tracked into hand-held and in-use shots, then checked frame by frame against the accuracy list.

  6. Motion graphics

    Floating Display animation, kinetic captions, spectrum light sweeps and the end card.

  7. Edit, sound and grade

    Beat-matched edit, sound design, licensed music and one colour grade across real and AI footage.

  8. Review rounds

    You review a watermarked cut with time-coded notes. I revise and send it back.

  9. Delivery

    Platform-ready exports with safe zones checked, burned-in captions and clearly named hook variants, ready to upload to Ads Manager.

Why product ads take more work than they look

AI generation doesn't produce one clip on request. It produces a set of takes, and most get rejected: a hand grips wrong, the light doesn't match, hair moves unnaturally, or something morphs mid-shot. For a product that has to stay accurate, the bar is higher still.

This project also needs a lot of image editing. Every product image used as a reference or composite has to be cut out, cleaned, relit and colour-matched to its scene. Many are reworked several times as the scenes change. Here is a typical ratio for an ad like this:

10–15generations per usable AI shot
80–120total clips generated to keep ~8
40+product images edited and composited
~840frames checked at 30fps for 28s

That ratio is built into every tier below. You pay for a finished, accurate ad, not per attempt.
